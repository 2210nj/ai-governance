# AI: A One-Page Explanation for a Business Leader

### What is Artificial Intelligence?

**Artificial Intelligence (AI) is technology that enables machines to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence**, such as understanding language, recognizing patterns, making predictions, recommending actions, or generating content.

From a business perspective, AI is not simply about "robots" or automation. It is about using data and intelligent systems to **improve decisions, increase efficiency, reduce costs, manage risk, and create better customer experiences.**

---

### How Does AI Create Business Value?

AI typically creates value in four ways:

| Business Objective | How AI Can Help | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Increase efficiency** | Automates repetitive or knowledge-intensive work | Summarizing documents or generating reports |
| **Improve decisions** | Identifies patterns and makes predictions | Predicting customer churn |
| **Improve customer experience** | Personalizes interactions and recommendations | Netflix recommending content |
| **Create new products/services** | Enables capabilities that weren't previously practical | AI-powered assistants |

The key question for a business leader is therefore:

> **"What business problem are we solving with AI, and is AI the right solution?"**

---

### AI vs Machine Learning vs Generative AI

Think of these as related concepts:

**AI** → The broad field of making machines perform intelligent tasks.

**Machine Learning (ML)** → A way of building AI systems that learn patterns from data rather than relying entirely on explicitly programmed rules.

**Generative AI** → AI that can create new content such as text, images, code, audio, or video.

For example:

**AI:** A system recommends products to a customer.

**ML:** The system learns from customers' previous behavior to improve those recommendations.

**Generative AI:** A chatbot explains why a particular product may be suitable for the customer.

---

### What Should a Business Leader Consider Before Adopting AI?

AI adoption should not start with:

> "Where can we use ChatGPT?"

It should start with:

> **"What business problem are we trying to solve?"**

Then consider:

1. **Business value** – Will it generate measurable value?
2. **Data** – Do we have appropriate, reliable, and legally usable data?
3. **Risk** – What happens if the AI gets something wrong?
4. **People** – Who will use it, review it, and be accountable?
5. **Compliance** – Are there regulatory or contractual requirements?
6. **Security & privacy** – Could sensitive information be exposed?
7. **Human oversight** – When should a person review or override the AI?
8. **Monitoring** – How will we know the AI continues to perform correctly?

---

### Why Does AI Governance Matter?

AI systems can create significant value, but they can also introduce risks such as:

- **Bias and discrimination**
- **Incorrect or misleading outputs**
- **Privacy violations**
- **Security risks**
- **Lack of transparency**
- **Regulatory non-compliance**
- **Reputational damage**
- **Over-reliance on automated decisions**

**AI Governance provides the framework for using AI responsibly while still enabling business innovation.**

A good AI Governance professional does **not** simply say "No" to risky AI.

They help the business answer:

> **"How can we use AI to achieve this objective while managing the associated risks?"**

---

### The Business Leader's AI Mindset

A mature organization moves from:

**"Can we use AI?"**

to:

**"Should we use AI?"**

and ultimately:

**"How do we use AI responsibly, measure its value, and manage its risks?"**

### In One Sentence

> **AI is a business capability that can augment people, automate work, improve decisions, and create new opportunities, but it needs appropriate governance to ensure that the value created outweighs the risks introduced.**
